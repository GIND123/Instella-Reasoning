\begin{abstract}
Large language models increasingly grade the outputs of other models, yet a judge is
itself a trained artifact whose competence may depend on whether the graded item
appeared in the judge's own training corpus. Existing audits characterise position
bias, verbosity bias, self preference, and generator to evaluator relatedness, none of
which establishes membership of the graded item, because the corpora of models normally
deployed as judges are not public. Judge accuracy is measured against exactly verified
membership. The AMD Instella family publishes a complete pretraining mixture whose stage
2 component derives from GSM8K train and not from GSM8K test, so membership of each
graded item follows from exact 13 gram containment against a 1.45 GB corpus rather than
from a membership inference attack. Difficulty matched arms hold 250 verified seen and
250 verified unseen problems, with a detector false positive rate of 0.3 percent
measured against 1000 known negatives. Three judges of increasing capability, namely
Instella 3B Instruct, Qwen2.5 7B Instruct, and Qwen2.5 14B Instruct, emit 48,408
verdicts at temperature 0 under two conditions differing only in whether the gold answer
appears in the judge prompt. Withholding the answer forces assessment of the presented
reasoning; supplying it reduces grading to comparison and serves as a control, since
recall of a solution cannot operate once the solution is given. A dissociation follows.
In the control condition the membership gap in balanced accuracy converges toward zero
as capability rises, moving from $-0.122$ to $-0.002$ to $+0.008$, while control
condition balanced accuracy reaches 0.94 to 0.97. Withholding the answer removes the
convergence: the gap runs $-0.081$, $-0.200$, and $-0.184$ across the same ladder,
emerging once a judge is competent enough to exhibit it and then holding. Intervals
derive from a bootstrap over parent problems, matching a measured intraclass correlation
of 0.48. The pattern is consistent with substitution of recall for verification, whereby
a remembered answer is compared against a candidate solution to a numerically perturbed
variant whose correct answer differs. Two negative controls carry methodological weight
beyond the finding: chance corrected agreement manufactures a large spurious membership
gap through unequal base rates, and a design restricted to a single 3 billion parameter
judge reverses the conclusion by sitting within 0.04 of chance in both conditions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A language model that grades another language model has been promoted,
quietly and with little argument, from examinee to examiner. The promotion
carries an assumption that is stated rarely and tested almost never: that a
grader's competence does not depend on the material placed in front of it.
An examiner who happens to have read the answer key is still, on that
assumption, an examiner.

Consider what follows if the assumption fails. A judge asked to rule on a
candidate solution to a problem it has already read during training may
produce a verdict that reports recall rather than assessment. The verdict
then becomes a measurement whose calibration varies with the very quantity
being measured, which is the one property no instrument is permitted to
have. Judges of exactly that kind now sit inside reinforcement learning from
human feedback, direct preference optimisation, and constitutional
pipelines, where a miscalibrated verdict does not merely mismeasure a model
but is written into its weights.

The assumption has gone untested for a mundane reason. Testing it requires
knowing what the judge read, and the models routinely deployed as judges
do not disclose a training corpus. Prior work has therefore studied what
could be studied. Zheng et al.~\citep{zheng2023judging} establish that
frontier judges agree with human preference above eighty percent of the
time while exhibiting position, verbosity, and self enhancement biases. Li
et al.~\citep{li2025preference} name preference leakage, a contamination
problem defined by kinship between the data generator and the evaluator,
and state explicitly that the mechanism is relatedness between models rather
than membership of the graded item in the evaluator's corpus. Norman et
al.~\citep{norman2026reliability} audit twenty one judges across roughly
541{,}000 judgments and report that high test retest reliability coexists
with severe position bias, which separates reliability from validity and
leaves the harder question open. Each study circles the same locked door.

The AMD Instella family~\citep{instella2025} leaves the door unlocked. AMD
publishes the complete pretraining mixture, and its stage 2 component
\texttt{amd/Instella-GSM8K-synthetic} is derived from GSM8K
train~\citep{cobbe2021} and not from GSM8K test. Membership of a GSM8K item
in the judge's training data therefore follows from exact string overlap
rather than from a membership inference attack of the kind developed by
Carlini et al.~\citep{carlini2023} and Shi et al.~\citep{shi2024}, whose
estimates Yang et al.~\citep{yang2023rephrased} show can be evaded outright
by rephrasing. The corpus offers a second and less obvious gift. Because it
is derived from train, every GSM8K test item is a known negative and cannot
be contaminated, so the error rate of any overlap detector becomes
measurable instead of arguable. The classical any n gram rule of Brown et
al.~\citep{brown2020} flags three of one thousand known negatives, a false
positive rate of 0.3 percent, and the threshold adopted here flags none.
Whatever else follows, it does not follow from a noisy treatment label.

One condition, however, would not have settled anything. A judge might
grade memorised problems differently for reasons that have nothing to do
with memory, and a single measurement cannot tell the two apart. The design
therefore holds everything fixed except a single sentence in the prompt.
Withholding the gold answer forces the judge to evaluate the reasoning
presented, and is the only setting in which recall could plausibly assist.
Supplying the gold answer reduces grading to comparison and functions as a
control, since recall of a solution cannot operate once the solution is
printed on the page. A memorisation account predicts a specific and
falsifiable shape: the effect must survive in the first condition and
collapse in the second. A distributional account predicts no such
asymmetry.

The shape appears. Across three judges of increasing capability, namely
Instella 3B Instruct, Qwen2.5 7B Instruct, and Qwen2.5 14B
Instruct~\citep{qwen2024}, and across 48{,}408 verdicts issued at
temperature 0, the membership gap in the control condition converges toward
zero as capability rises, moving from $-0.122$ to $-0.002$ to $+0.008$
while balanced accuracy climbs to between 0.94 and 0.97. In the reference
free condition no such convergence occurs. The gap runs $-0.081$, $-0.200$,
and $-0.184$ along the same ladder, appearing once a judge is competent
enough to exhibit it and holding thereafter. A competent judge grades
verifiably memorised problems substantially less accurately, and only when
denied the answer.

Two near misses deserve reporting alongside the finding, because either
would have been published had the corresponding control been omitted.
Measured as chance corrected agreement, the membership gap was large and
excluded zero everywhere, an artifact of unequal ground truth base rates
across the arms rather than of judge behaviour. Measured on the 3 billion
parameter judge alone, the gap appeared in both conditions and would have
supported the opposite conclusion, because a judge sitting within 0.04 of
chance in every configuration reports its own noise floor rather than a
property of judging. The control and the capability ladder are jointly load
bearing, and a design carrying only one of them arrives at the wrong answer
with confidence.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Judge accuracy conditioned on verified rather than inferred
        membership.} Across 48{,}408 verdicts, the graded item's presence in a
        published pretraining corpus is established by exact 13 gram containment,
        and the detector's own false positive rate is measured at 0.0 percent
        against one thousand items that cannot be contaminated
        (Section~\ref{sec:membership}).
  \item \textbf{A prompt level dissociation that isolates recall from difficulty.}
        Withholding versus supplying the gold answer changes one sentence and
        nothing else, so the membership gap surviving in one condition and
        collapsing in the other excludes the distributional explanations a single
        condition design cannot address (Sections~\ref{sec:conditions}
        and~\ref{sec:dissociation}).
  \item \textbf{A capability ladder showing the effect strengthens with
        competence.} The reference free gap emerges only once a judge grades
        reliably, reaching $-0.200$ and $-0.184$ at 7B and 14B, which is the
        opposite of the shrinking-with-scale signature of a small model artifact
        (Section~\ref{sec:dissociation}).
  \item \textbf{Two negative controls reported as findings, not footnotes.}
        Cohen's kappa manufactures a spurious membership gap of up to $-0.292$
        with intervals excluding zero, and a design using only the 3B judge
        reverses the conclusion. Both are retained as methodological cautions for
        studies of this form (Sections~\ref{sec:metric} and~\ref{sec:competence}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verified Membership}
\label{sec:membership}

\subsection{Exact containment against a published training corpus}
Membership is measured against Instella's stage 2 pretraining corpus,
\texttt{amd/Instella-GSM8K-synthetic} (1.45~GB, ${\sim}1.4$ million synthetic documents),
which is derived strictly from the GSM8K training split~\citep{cobbe2021}. Before indexing,
a normalisation pipeline strips punctuation and folds case, reducing all text to lowercase
alphanumeric tokens (\texttt{[a-z0-9]+}). Following the GPT-3 contamination
methodology~\citep{brown2020}, each prompt is decomposed into a sliding window of 13
consecutive tokens. Containment is computed in a single streaming pass using a rolling
polynomial hash modulo a 61-bit Mersenne prime ($2^{61}-1$) with base $1{,}000{,}003$, so
cost is linear in corpus tokens and memory is linear in item windows. Containment $c$ is the
fraction of an item's 13-gram windows found in the corpus. Items are labelled SEEN if
$c \ge 0.80$ and UNSEEN if $c \le 0.10$; items in between are excluded from both arms rather
than assigned to one, on the principle that an indefensible treatment label costs more than a
smaller sample. From the eligible pools the two arms are drawn as 250 seen and 250 unseen
problems, matched on a three-way difficulty stratification (84/83/83 items per stratum) so
that arm composition cannot substitute for a membership effect.

\subsection{Detector false positive rate on one thousand known negatives}
Because the corpus derives strictly from the training split, the 1{,}000 items of the GSM8K
test split are guaranteed true negatives, and the false positive rate of any threshold
becomes measurable rather than arguable. Table~\ref{tab:fpr_audit} reports the audit for
three rules. The permissive any-match rule of Brown et al.~\citep{brown2020} flags 3 of
1{,}000 known negatives; the threshold adopted here flags none. Test-item containment has
median 0.000, 99th percentile 0.000, and maximum 0.538. At a measured 0.0 percent false
positive rate on known negatives, treatment label noise is not an available explanation for
any result below.

\subsection{Identifier namespacing and a containment measurement failure}
GSM8K train and test share an identical identifier schema, \texttt{gsm8k\_NNNNN}, so loading
1{,}000 items from each collides on every identifier. Keying the 13-gram match index by bare
identifier lets match counts from disjoint splits accumulate into a shared bucket. Because
the denominator window count is fixed per prompt while the numerator accumulates across
splits, containment, a fraction bounded above by unity, reached 7.78. Prepending split
namespaces (\texttt{train::gsm8k\_00001}) isolates the buckets and restores $c \in [0,1]$.
The failure is reported because any replication of this design that loads both splits will
encounter it, and because it silently invalidates treatment assignment rather than crashing.

\section{Judge Protocol}

\subsection{Graded units, checkpoints, and variant composition}
The 250 seen and 250 unseen parent problems are expanded into 2{,}017 variant rows. Each
parent yields one original, two answer-preserving structural rewrites (rephrasing, distractor
insertion), and two answer-changing numeric perturbations in the style of
GSM-Symbolic~\citep{mirzadeh2025}; rows failing validation are dropped, leaving 1{,}484
answer-preserving rows and 533 answer-changing rows. Separating the two variant types is what
makes movement within an arm interpretable: an answer-preserving rewrite leaves the gold
answer intact, so any change in judge accuracy across it is judge fragility, not task
difficulty.

A graded unit is a triple $(x, y, \hat{v})$, where $x$ is the variant prompt, $y$ is the
candidate solution, and $\hat{v} \in \{\textsc{correct}, \textsc{incorrect}, \textsc{abstain}\}$
is the judge's verdict. Candidate solutions are generated by four checkpoints spanning the
Instella-3B training lifespan: \texttt{stage1} (base), \texttt{stage2} (after
\texttt{amd/Instella-GSM8K-synthetic} enters training), \texttt{sft}, and \texttt{instruct}.
Generation is greedy in bfloat16 at a fixed batch size of 8 under
\texttt{transformers==4.56.0} pinned exactly. Both controls are load bearing rather than
incidental: a version range would place different checkpoints on different minor versions and
enter the estimate as though it were a model difference, and greedy bf16 decoding is sensitive
to padding, so a change in batch size alters tie breaking.

\subsection{The reference free condition and the reference based control}
\label{sec:conditions}
The two conditions differ only in whether the gold answer appears in the judge prompt. In the
\emph{reference free} condition, the primary test setting, the gold answer is withheld and the
judge must evaluate the candidate's step-by-step reasoning; this is the only setting in which
recall of a memorised solution could plausibly assist. In the \emph{reference based} condition
the gold answer for the specific variant is supplied, reducing grading to comparison. The
latter is the control: an effect appearing there cannot be recall of the solution, because the
solution is on the page.

The contrast is what carries the inference. A memorisation account predicts that the seen-item
penalty persists under reference free grading and collapses toward zero under reference based
grading. An account based on item difficulty or on distributional differences between the arms
predicts the penalty in both conditions, because neither the items nor their difficulty change
between them.

\subsection{Verdict parsing and abstention accounting}
Judges emit a verdict at temperature 0 under a 24-token budget. Extraction applies a
negative-first substring rule, testing for \textsc{incorrect} before \textsc{correct}, since
the latter is a substring of the former and a naive parser silently inverts every negative
verdict. A response committing to neither class is recorded as an abstention rather than as a
disagreement; conflating the two inflates apparent reliability. Abstention rates range from
0.0 to 2.6 percent (Appendix~\ref{app:figures}).

\subsection{Balanced accuracy, and why chance corrected agreement fails here}
\label{sec:metric}
Judge accuracy was first measured as Cohen's kappa against exact-match ground truth. The
result appeared decisive and was wrong: the membership gap ran from $-0.168$ to $-0.292$ with
intervals excluding zero. Kappa is a function of the marginal distributions as well as of the
agreement, and the arms have very different ground-truth base rates: \texttt{instruct} is
correct on 77 percent of seen rows against 53 percent of unseen rows. An arm whose base rate
sits near one half has substantially more kappa headroom than a skewed arm, so the skew alone
generates a gap even when judge decision quality is identical. This is the kappa
paradox~\citep{feinstein1990}, a known property of the statistic rather than a finding of this
work; it is reported because it produced a publishable-looking result on this data.

All results below use \textbf{balanced accuracy}, the unweighted mean of sensitivity and
specificity. Both quantities condition on the true class, so neither depends on the base rate,
their mean is comparable across arms by construction, and chance is 0.50 regardless of skew.

\subsection{Cluster robust inference over parent problems}
The 2{,}017 rows are not 2{,}017 independent observations: rows derived from the same parent
problem are strongly correlated, with a measured intraclass correlation of approximately 0.48.
At a mean cluster size of 4.0 rows this implies a design effect of roughly 2.4, so resampling
rows would understate standard errors by about that factor and contract intervals accordingly.
All intervals therefore come from a bootstrap with 4{,}000 resamples that draws \emph{parent
problems} as indivisible clusters.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Judge competence is demonstrated, not assumed}
\label{sec:competence}
An instrument claim requires an instrument that works, so baseline competence is measured
rather than assumed (Table~\ref{tab:dissociation}). The Instella-3B-Instruct judge sits within
0.04 of chance on the seen arm in every configuration, including when handed the reference
answer, so its rows describe a noise floor rather than a judge. The 7B and 14B judges reach
0.9370 to 0.9728 balanced accuracy in the reference based control
(Figure~\ref{fig:judge_ladder}). The substantive claims below
therefore rest on demonstrably competent instruments, and the 3B rows are retained only as the
negative control described in Section~\ref{sec:dissociation}.

\subsection{The dissociation across the capability ladder}
\label{sec:dissociation}
In the reference based control the membership gap converges toward zero as capability rises:
$-0.0541$, $-0.0359$, $-0.0230$ on \texttt{instruct} outputs, and $-0.1219$, $-0.0024$,
$+0.0077$ on \texttt{stage2} outputs, with the final intervals spanning zero in both cases.
In the reference free condition it does not converge. The two competent judges show gaps of
$-0.2000$ and $-0.1840$, with 95 percent cluster bootstrap intervals excluding zero
(Table~\ref{tab:dissociation}, Figure~\ref{fig:judge_dissociation}).

Three readings follow. Capability is real: given the reference answer, the larger judges are
strong, so conclusions about the instrument come from instruments that work. The control
behaves as a control: supplying the correct answer for the specific variant removes the effect,
which is what a memorisation account predicts and a distributional account does not, since the
items are identical across conditions. And scale does not rescue the reference free gap, which
grows once the judge is competent enough to exhibit it and then holds; a small-model artifact
would shrink instead. A design restricted to the 3B judge would have found a gap in both
conditions, $-0.0541$ and $-0.0809$, and concluded the opposite.

\subsection{Reliability per design cell, and abstention as a separate series}
Decomposing accuracy across the cells of the membership $\times$ perturbation design separates
prompt fragility from task difficulty. Answer-preserving rewrites leave the gold answer intact,
so movement between original and rewritten prompts within an arm is judge fragility by
construction. Under reference free grading on seen problems, accuracy instead drops sharply on
the answer-changing perturbations, which is where a remembered answer would be wrong
(Figure~\ref{fig:judge_cells}). Abstentions are tracked as a separate series rather than folded
into disagreement, since selective abstention can inflate accuracy by discarding hard cases;
rates stay between 0.0 and 2.6 percent across all 48{,}408 verdicts, so the balanced accuracy
figures are not an artifact of it (Figure~\ref{fig:judge_abstention}).

\subsection{Mechanism: recall substituted for verification}
The pattern is consistent with the judge substituting retrieval of a remembered answer for
verification of the reasoning presented. Answer-changing perturbations have different correct
answers from the memorised original, so a judge comparing against recall marks valid
calculations wrong, and the degradation should concentrate on exactly those 533 rows, which is
where Figure~\ref{fig:judge_cells} places it. Supplying the target answer bypasses the lookup
and collapses the seen-item penalty. The account is inferential, derived from the experimental
dissociation rather than from training data attribution; confirming it directly would require
influence-function attribution~\citep{koh2017,pruthi2020,grosse2023} linking a verdict to a
specific memorised document.

\section{Threats to Validity}

\paragraph{Membership is verified for one judge and assumed for two.}
Membership is established against Instella's published corpus, so the Instella judge has
verified membership while the Qwen judges are graded against a corpus that is not theirs. The
Qwen results establish that a competent judge exhibits the dissociation on this item partition,
but membership in Qwen's own corpus is unknown. This is a genuine asymmetry: the strongest
evidence comes from the judges whose membership cannot be verified. Resolving it requires a
second open-corpus family of comparable capability.

\paragraph{One model family provides the candidate solutions.}
All graded outputs come from Instella-3B checkpoints. Whether the dissociation reproduces on
candidate solutions from other families is untested.

\paragraph{Two conditions, one prompt template.}
Both conditions use a single standardised template (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}). Sensitivity of
the dissociation to judge prompt phrasing, instruction wording, or formatting is untested, and
a prompt-perturbation sweep is the most direct extension of this work.

\paragraph{The null after supervised fine tuning is unexplained.}
The effect concentrates on \texttt{stage2} and \texttt{instruct} outputs. The \texttt{stage1}
null is accounted for: those outputs are wrong almost everywhere, at 7 percent accuracy, and are
therefore trivially gradeable in either condition. The \texttt{sft} null is not. Its outputs are
of comparable quality to \texttt{instruct}, yet it shows no membership gap in either condition,
and the present data do not explain why.

\section{Conclusion}
LLM judge reliability is not independent of the material judged. On problems whose presence in
a training corpus is established by exact overlap rather than inferred, a competent judge grades
substantially less accurately, and the degradation appears only when the reference answer is
withheld. The reference based control converging toward zero while the reference free gap
persists is what separates this finding from the distributional explanations a single condition
design cannot exclude.

The two negative controls carry methodological weight beyond the specific result. Cohen's kappa
manufactured a large and interval-excluding spurious effect through base-rate skew, and a single
3 billion parameter judge produced the opposite conclusion by reporting its own noise floor in
both conditions. Studies of this form require a base-rate-invariant metric, cluster-robust
inference over the correct unit, and an instrument whose competence is demonstrated rather than
assumed.

\section{Related work in full}
\label{app:related}

\paragraph{Judge reliability and bias.}
Zheng et al.~\citep{zheng2023judging} introduce MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena, report that frontier
judges reach above 80 percent agreement with human preference, and identify position, verbosity,
and self-enhancement biases. Norman et al.~\citep{norman2026reliability} extend this to a
systematic audit across 21 judges and three benchmarks using Cohen's kappa, Krippendorff's alpha,
position bias measured as deviation of paired outcome probability from one half, and test-retest
reliability. They report same-verdict rates above 95 percent at temperature 0, falling to roughly
70 percent at temperature 1, which is why the judge is held at temperature 0 throughout this work:
a sampled judge would confound the membership contrast with its own decoding noise.

\paragraph{Contamination in judging.}
Li et al.~\citep{li2025preference} define preference leakage as bias arising when the data
generator and the evaluator are the same model, share an inheritance relationship, or belong to
one family. The mechanism is relatedness between models, not membership of the evaluated item in
the evaluator's training data, and the paper does not test the latter. The present design holds
the generator family fixed and varies membership instead, which is the complementary contrast.

\paragraph{Contamination detection.}
The n-gram overlap family originates with Brown et al.~\citep{brown2020}, who flag an item when
any of its n-grams appears in the corpus. Perplexity-based membership inference is developed by
Carlini et al.~\citep{carlini2023} and Shi et al.~\citep{shi2024}. Yang
et al.~\citep{yang2023rephrased} show that n-gram filtering misses rephrased contamination
entirely. All of these \emph{estimate} membership; the present setting \emph{establishes} it,
which is what permits the detector's false positive rate to be measured rather than assumed.

\paragraph{Reasoning robustness.}
Mirzadeh et al.~\citep{mirzadeh2025} introduce GSM-Symbolic and hypothesise that models replicate
reasoning steps seen in training rather than reasoning, reporting drops of up to 65 percent when
an irrelevant clause is added. Ivanova~\citep{ivanova2024} challenges the statistical basis,
finding that for 21 of 25 models there is insufficient evidence to reject equality of performance
between GSM8K and GSM-Symbolic. The perturbation apparatus here follows the former; the
statistical posture, in particular the refusal to read a point estimate whose interval spans zero,
follows the latter.

The full grid is three judges $\times$ four target checkpoints $\times$ two conditions, giving 24

balanced-accuracy figures and the discarded Cohen's kappa figures are released in
\texttt{analysis/judge\_reliability.json}, so the rejected metric remains inspectable alongside

\section{Judge prompt templates for both conditions}
\label{app:prompts}
Both conditions share every token except the reference block. The judge is instructed to emit a
single word and is decoded greedily under a 24-token budget.

\paragraph{Reference free.}
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
You are grading a math solution. Problem: \{problem\}\\
Proposed solution: \{solution\}\\
Is the proposed solution correct? Answer with exactly one word, CORRECT or INCORRECT.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Reference based (control).}
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
You are grading a math solution. Problem: \{problem\}\\
Correct answer: \{gold\}\\
Proposed solution: \{solution\}\\
Is the proposed solution correct? Answer with exactly one word, CORRECT or INCORRECT.
\end{quote}

Parsing tests for \texttt{INCORRECT} before \texttt{CORRECT}, since the latter is a substring of
the former. Responses matching neither are recorded as abstentions.

\section{Reproduction, artifacts, and compute}
\label{app:repro}
Judge grading consumed 1.51 GPU-hours on a single A100 40GB for all 48{,}408 verdicts.
Candidate-solution generation across the four checkpoints is reported separately in the
accompanying artifact release. The full pipeline is driven by three commands:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
modal run experiments/modal\_fullscale.py::judge\_run --tag instruct \\
\hspace*{2em}--mode reference\_free --judge-model "Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct" \\
modal run experiments/modal\_fullscale.py::judge\_analysis --write \\
modal run experiments/modal\_fullscale.py::supplementary\_figures
\end{quote}

Raw verdicts, generations, exact-match scores, containment measurements, analysis artifacts, and
all figures are released under \texttt{experiments/runs/fullscale-S250-v2}. Determinism controls:
\texttt{transformers} pinned to 4.56.0, greedy bfloat16 decoding, batch size fixed at 8, variants
seeded per (item, perturbation type) so variant text is stable regardless of call order, and every
generation batch flushed and fsynced with resume keyed on item identifier.

\paragraph{Anonymisation.}
Review is double blind. The artifact repository and dataset are hosted at an anonymised mirror for
the review period; the de-anonymised links will be substituted in the camera-ready version.

\section{Broader impact}
\label{app:impact}
The finding is a caution about measurement rather than a capability advance. LLM judges are
already embedded in preference optimisation pipelines, where a verdict is not merely reported but
written into model weights. A judge that degrades on exactly the benchmark items most likely to
appear in public training corpora will bias those pipelines in a direction that standard judge
audits, which do not condition on membership, are not designed to detect. The practical
recommendation is narrow and cheap: where a candidate reference answer exists, supply it, since
the control condition here shows the membership gap collapsing when it is present. No new models,
datasets, or capabilities are released; the artifacts are evaluation records over public
benchmarks and a publicly released pretraining corpus.
