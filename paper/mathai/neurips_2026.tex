\begin{abstract}
Whether a language model can tell that a problem does not determine an answer has been
measured on released models, which cannot say when the ability arrives in training or what
takes it away. Instella publishes intermediate checkpoints spanning pretraining and post
training together with its full data mixture, so both questions become measurable. A premise
deletion probe removes exactly one premise the reference solution consumes, giving 3179
variants over 1779 parent problems, and every abstention rate is read against an answerable
control on the untouched parents, where declining is an error. Verification is close to absent
before post training and is built by it: discrimination runs $+1.84$, $+10.10$, $+38.08$ and
$+52.63$ points across stage one, stage two, supervised fine tuning and instruct, while wrong
refusals stay below 3.5 percent throughout, and a reworded licence preserves the ladder. An
independently built MATH probe reproduces it, and three instruct checkpoints from other
families all discriminate. Reasoning depth
then erodes what training builds. Abstention on underdetermined problems falls $5.22$ points
per additional calculator step while the answerable control stays flat at $-0.11$, a
difference of $-5.11$ on $[-6.36, -3.82]$, and the decay steepens as the checkpoint improves.
Under the answer forcing prompt used for benchmark scoring the strongest checkpoint still
answers 97.6 percent of provably underdetermined items. Controlled injection at a fixed token
budget drives verbatim reproduction to 0.946 and returns the memorised calculation chain, at
$+5.44$ on $[+1.46, +9.38]$, without returning the answer, at $+1.52$ on $[-2.12, +5.21]$, so
what depth erodes is not recall of training text.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A model that answers a grade school arithmetic problem has been given premises that determine
an answer. Whether it checked that they do is invisible to the score, because every item on a
benchmark is answerable. Probing that directly requires items that are not, and a growing body
of work now supplies them. QuestBench casts underspecified reasoning tasks as constraint
satisfaction problems with a missing variable~\citep{li2025questbench}, AbstentionBench
assembles unanswerable questions across domains and reports that reasoning fine tuning
degrades abstention~\citep{kirichenko2025abstentionbench}, PCBench evaluates premise critique
across fifteen models and finds that most detect a flawed premise only when explicitly
prompted to look~\citep{li2025premisecritique}, and ReliableMath does the same for unsolvable
mathematics~\citep{xue2025reliablemath}. That models often fail to verify their premises, and
do better when told they may decline, is therefore established rather than at issue here.

What that work cannot ask is developmental. Every model it evaluates is a finished artifact,
so the capability can be measured but not located: nothing says whether verification arrives
with scale, with instruction tuning, or with preference optimisation, because the intermediate
checkpoints of the models under test are not released. Instella~\citep{instella2025} publishes
four checkpoints spanning its pipeline along with the complete data mixture, which makes the
question answerable on one family. This paper asks when premise verification appears, and what
it is lost to.

Both answers turn out to concern reasoning rather than compliance. Verification is close to
absent in the pretrained checkpoints and is manufactured by post training, rising from $+1.84$
to $+52.63$ points of discrimination against an answerable control. It then erodes with the
depth of the chain the model is tracking, falling $5.22$ points per additional reasoning step
while the control stays flat, and eroding faster in the checkpoints that verify best. Because
the corpus is published, the erosion can also be separated from memorisation: continued
pretraining that drives verbatim reproduction of injected documents to 0.946 returns the
memorised calculation chain without returning the answer, so what fails at depth is the check,
not the recall.

\section{Setup}

\paragraph{Exposure.} Containment is the fraction of an item's thirteen grams appearing in a
single corpus document, taking the maximum over documents. Measured on identical items
through one extraction path, 9.65 percent of GSM8K train parents reach containment 0.999 or
above against the 119{,}014 row subset consumed during stage two and 18.52 percent against
the full pool, so the pool overstates exposure and the subset is the correct reference.

\paragraph{The probe.} Each item removes one premise the reference solution consumes,
verified by requiring the annotated calculator chain to depend on it. The result is 3179
variants over 1779 parent problems, split 1591 train and 1588 test. Every item is
underdetermined by construction, so declining is the correct response and any specific number
is wrong.

\paragraph{Checkpoints.} Four public Instella checkpoints span the pipeline: stage one, which
precedes the synthetic corpus, stage two, supervised fine tuning and instruct. Decoding is
greedy and held fixed; base checkpoints are prompted with four exemplars and no chat
template, since a base model loops without them.

\paragraph{Inference.} Intervals are cluster bootstraps over parent problems with 4000 draws
at seed 6198, because several variants can descend from one problem and resampling rows
would understate the interval.

\section{Verification of premise sufficiency}

\subsection{Post training builds the capability}

Under the answer forcing prompt used for benchmark scoring, the instruct checkpoint declines
on 2.4 percent of items that provably do not determine an answer and commits to one on the
remaining 97.6 percent, or 99.7 percent counting any emitted number; the three earlier
checkpoints commit on 99.5 percent or more. Naming \texttt{\#\#\#\# unanswerable} as
an available response, and changing nothing else, raises declining to 53.8 percent at the
instruct checkpoint. That rate alone is ambiguous, since every probe item is underdetermined
and a model offered an escape hatch may simply take it. Running the identical prompt over the
untouched parent problems, which are answerable and where declining is an error, gives 1.1
percent. The difference of $+52.63$ points on $[+50.77, +54.57]$ is the quantity of interest:
declining tracks whether the problem can actually be answered.

Table~\ref{tab:abstain} shows the capability developing across the pipeline. Stage one, which
precedes the synthetic corpus entirely, discriminates by less than two points. Each
subsequent stage improves it, and wrong refusals on answerable problems never exceed 3.5
percent, so the gain does not come from a growing willingness to decline.

A second control rules out the remaining reading, that a pruned problem reads oddly and the
model responds to perturbed text. Inserting an irrelevant twenty word sentence carrying an
unused number leaves a problem fully answerable while perturbing its surface. Declining moves
from 0.0113 clean to 0.0170 with an off topic insertion and 0.0125 with an in domain one,
against 0.538 when a premise is actually removed. Rewording the licence while holding its
information and its token fixed preserves the ladder, at $+0.80$, $+6.64$, $+33.41$ and
$+43.77$ points with every interval excluding zero, and moves the level: Appendix~\ref{app:reword}
reports both.

\subsection{Reasoning depth erodes it}

The capability is not uniform over problems. Figure~\ref{fig:abstain} plots abstention
against the length of the reference solution's calculator chain. On underdetermined problems
at the instruct checkpoint the rate falls from 0.623 at one step to 0.364 at seven, a slope
of $-5.22$ points per step. On the answerable control the slope is $-0.11$ with an interval
containing zero, so the pattern is not a tendency to decline more on short problems. The
difference of slopes is $-5.11$ on $[-6.36, -3.82]$.

The decay steepens as the checkpoint improves, running $-2.34$, $-3.64$ and $-5.11$ for
stage two, supervised fine tuning and instruct. Two alternatives are ruled out. Truncation
never exceeds 0.7 percent at any chain length and restricting to terminated generations
reproduces the rates exactly. Difficulty is separated by splitting parents on whether the
model answers the intact problem correctly, which it does for 76.4 percent: the slope is
$-4.93$ points per step on $[-6.51, -3.36]$ where it can solve the problem and $-4.36$ on
$[-6.54, -2.19]$ where it cannot, so chain depth carries information difficulty does not
account for.

\subsection{Both results hold on a second domain and in other families}

Two further sweeps address the single benchmark and single family. A premise deletion probe
built independently on MATH~\citep{hendrycks2021math}, 818 parents and 1091 variants drawn
from all seven subjects under the same two validity checks, reproduces the ladder: decision quality
runs 0.516, 0.557, 0.611 and 0.675 across the same checkpoints, against 0.509, 0.550, 0.690
and 0.763 on GSM8K. The shape is the same and the level is lower, which is expected, since
competition problems are harder and a model declining one it simply cannot solve inflates the
answerable rate and shrinks the measured gap. The depth decay reproduces there too, at
$-6.41$ points per additional premise sentence on $[-8.43, -4.49]$, steepening across the
pipeline as on GSM8K. The MATH control arm was built without a depth variable, so this
replicates the gradient but not its control.

Across families, three further instruct checkpoints graded the same GSM8K items under the
same prompt. All four discriminate, with decision quality of 0.763 for Instella 3B Instruct,
0.795 and 0.759 for Qwen2.5 at 3B and 1.5B, and 0.573 for OLMo-2 1B Instruct, so premise
verification is a property of instruction tuned small language models rather than of one
pipeline. The spread is not a ranking, since the models differ in pretraining corpus,
instruction tuning recipe and training compute, and nothing in the design attributes a few
points to any of those. OLMo-2 is the informative case: its discrimination of $+14.68$ points
on $[+12.13, +17.23]$ clears zero, yet it declines 29.2 percent of answerable problems, so its
decisions sit near chance. A difference of rates would call that a weaker version of the same
capability; the decision metric identifies it as declining freely rather than selectively,
which is why false refusal is reported alongside discrimination throughout.

\section{What exposure returns}

\subsection{Continued pretraining at controlled doses}

Whether exposure supplies the answer is causal, so selected items were injected by continued
pretraining at five doses under a fixed 8{,}388{,}608 token budget, held constant so a higher
dose displaces filler rather than buying compute. Every arm
carries positive controls evaluated before and after: injected document loss must fall and
the model must continue an injected document verbatim, since a flat dose response with failed
controls would measure a broken training run. Injection worked, verbatim reproduction
climbing from 0.133 to 0.946 while held out loss rises, so the mixture taught those documents
rather than the task.

\subsection{The chain returns, the answer does not}

Against a never injected control at the same dose, reproduction of the memorised calculation
chain reaches $+5.44$ points on $[+1.46, +9.38]$ at sixty four repetitions and rises with
dose, while reproduction of the final answer reaches $+1.52$ on $[-2.12, +5.21]$, an interval
containing zero. The model reproduces the source text almost exactly and still does not
recover the deleted quantity or the answer it determines. Repeating the identical mixture,
budget, seed and probe on OLMo-2-1B, Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-3B gives $+0.28$, $+0.00$ and
$+2.28$ points at the top dose, every interval containing zero, across ten dose points.
Qwen2.5-3B is the sharpest case, driven to verbatim reproduction of 1.000 and still
recovering the deleted answer no more often than a model that never saw the documents. Chain
reproduction is scored on three of the four models, and is established on Instella and
underpowered on the other two, as Appendix~\ref{app:dose} sets out.

\section{Discussion}

Two capabilities that a single accuracy number conflates come apart here. Executing a
solution procedure is present early and improves smoothly; verifying that the premises
support a solution at all appears later and degrades with the depth of the chain being
tracked. Benchmark scoring cannot see the difference, because every item it scores is
answerable. Reporting declining under a prompt that permits it, against an answerable
control, costs one evaluation pass and makes the second capability visible.

\paragraph{Limitations.} One problem generator for the injection arm. Detection is
measured through an explicit abstention option, so a model that recognises insufficiency but
lacks the vocabulary to say so is scored as failing. Difficulty is stratified by the model's own
accuracy on intact problems rather than by an independent measure, and depth on MATH is
sentence count rather than a verified calculation chain, which that benchmark does not
annotate. Chain reproduction under injection is established on one model, and the
replication is underpowered for it. Attribution to specific training documents is not
established anywhere.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} Measured against exposure fixed by containment rather than
estimated, Instella's grade school arithmetic performance is not recovered text: no test item
approaches containment against the corpus, and heavy controlled injection returns the
memorised calculation chain without returning the answer. What the probe reveals instead is a
separable capability. The model detects that a problem no longer determines an answer, does
so increasingly well across training, and loses that detection as the reasoning chain
lengthens.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Premise verification and abstention.} That models answer questions which do not
determine an answer is well documented. QuestBench formalises underspecified reasoning as a
constraint satisfaction problem missing one variable and finds that models fail to identify
the missing variable even where they solve the fully specified
problem~\citep{li2025questbench}. AbstentionBench gathers unanswerable questions across
domains and reports that reasoning fine tuning degrades abstention rather than improving
it~\citep{kirichenko2025abstentionbench}. PCBench evaluates premise critique over fifteen
models and finds that most detect a flawed premise only under explicit prompting, which is
the same licensing dependence measured in Section 3.1~\citep{li2025premisecritique}, and
ReliableMath establishes the corresponding benchmark for unsolvable
mathematics~\citep{xue2025reliablemath}. Concurrent work sharpens two pieces of this. Gu et
al.~\citep{gu2026bridging} name the gap between detecting insufficiency and acting on it and
train an explicit answerability decision to close it; Wagner~\citep{wagner2026twoaxes}
separates confidence in an answer from confidence that a question is answerable and shows
the usual confidence signal is nearly blind to the second. The present work differs in what
it varies. Those studies evaluate released models, so they can establish that the capability
is uneven but not where it comes from or what removes it. Measuring four checkpoints of one
published pipeline turns the capability into a developmental quantity, and reports two
gradients rather than a level: it is manufactured almost entirely by post training, and it
decays with the depth of the reasoning chain, faster in the checkpoints that verify best.
The AbstentionBench finding that reasoning fine tuning degrades abstention and the ladder
reported here are not in conflict on this reading, since the depth gradient is a within
checkpoint effect that a single benchmark average over problems of mixed depth would
partially absorb.

\paragraph{Memorisation and contamination.} Huang et al.~\citep{huang2025mathperturb}
construct hand authored hard perturbations of competition mathematics and attribute accuracy
loss under an unchanged solution method to blind application of a learned procedure, a
reading broadly consistent with the procedure level memorization in Section 4.2, established
here against a corpus verified exactly rather than inferred. Wu et
al.~\citep{wu2025reasoning} show that reinforcement learning gains on mathematical reasoning
depend on the pretraining exposure of the base policy and argue that clean synthetic problems
are needed for reliable conclusions, a concern the checkpoint based design addresses by
evaluating a policy before any relevant exposure occurred. Zhang et
al.~\citep{zhang2024careful} rebuild GSM8K from a contamination free procedure and report
accuracy gaps of up to 13 points that track prior exposure across closed models, using an
estimated exposure signal in the absence of any released training corpus; the present work
substitutes an exact containment measurement for that estimate on a family where the corpus
is public.

\section{Containment method, calibration, and corpus routes}

The within split contamination contrast, high against low containment GSM8K train
items, was closed rather than merely complicated by two findings. The corpus subset
actually consumed during Instella 3B stage two pretraining, \texttt{train\_119K}, is a
119{,}014 row subset of a 1{,}367{,}882 row released pool; containment computed against
the pool over counts exposure, since 12.57 percent of GSM8K train reaches 0.80 or
higher against the subset actually consumed against 23.34 percent against the pool, both
measured on the same 891 parent problems through one extraction path. No GSM8K test item
reaches containment 0.50 against either corpus, and the maximum over the full pool is
0.487, so the released corpus is train derived and the split remains a usable exposure
contrast.
Independently, \texttt{allenai/tulu-3-sft-mixture} is itself part of the Instella stage
two pretraining mixture and \texttt{nvidia/OpenMathInstruct-2} enters at supervised fine
tuning; both are generated in part from GSM8K train, and both carry it at containment
0.999 or higher on more than 97 percent of items. Every GSM8K train item therefore
entered post stage one training through at least one of these routes regardless of its
containment in the Instella specific synthetic corpus, so a high against low contrast
defined on that corpus alone would compare two groups that are both almost entirely
exposed once these mixtures are accounted for; the contrast is not attenuated by this,
it is unidentifiable.

Item text is normalized to lowercase alphanumeric tokens and decomposed into every window
of 13 consecutive tokens, following the overlap convention of
Brown et al.~\citep{brown2020fewshot}. Containment against a document is the fraction of
an item's windows found in that document; containment against a corpus is the maximum of
that fraction over every document, computed in one streaming pass with an inverted index
from window to source item so cost scales linearly in corpus size. Because the corpus is
generated only from the GSM8K training split, every GSM8K test item is a verified
negative, and the false positive rate is measured rather than assumed: at the adopted
threshold of 0.80, zero of 1319 test items are flagged, a Wilson 95 percent upper bound
of 0.29 percent; a looser rule flagging any window match flags 3 of 1000 sampled test
items, 0.30 percent.

Within the Tulu 3 mixture, a sampled prefix of 300{,}000 rows, six of the mixture's
subsets, finds GSM8K train items reaching containment 0.999 through the
\texttt{flan\_v2\_converted} subset, 6021 of 7473 items from 89{,}982 rows of that source,
and through \texttt{wildchat} conversation logs, 4 items from 100{,}000 rows, an organic
route in which users pasted benchmark problems into chat. Every scan reports rows
examined alongside items found, since a sampled prefix bounds containment from below and
a zero means absent from the rows scanned rather than absent from the corpus.

\section{The irrelevance marker}
\label{app:marker}

The inserted frame in the crossed design ends "and has no bearing on this question", which
is an instruction to ignore the insertion carried inside the insertion itself.
Shi et al.~\citep{shi2023distracted} report that such an instruction mitigates the loss
irrelevant context causes, so the modest cost measured in Section 3.3 may sit inside their
mitigated regime rather than in tension with it. Testing that requires removing the clause
and nothing else. The replacement, "on the afternoon of the same day", holds the sentence at
exactly twenty words and preserves position, topic noun and unused integer.

\section{The two withdrawn checks, and the dose response in full}
\label{app:dose}

The clause ladder is an externally authored perturbation suite whose rungs share template
identifiers, which invites pairing each perturbed item against its own template head. The
instances at each rung are independently generated rather than derived from one another, so
a later rung is a different and harder problem set rather than the same problems with
clauses appended, and an accuracy difference across rungs therefore mixes added content with
added difficulty. Premise reordering permutes the non question sentences of a problem and
preserves the gold answer by construction, but a narrative word problem often carries
pronouns, temporal connectives and back references across sentences, so a permutation can
leave text that no reader could solve. Filtering to items where no premise after the first
carries such a dependency retains 24 percent of the set, and on that subset the effect is
$+2.2$ points on $[-6.5, +10.9]$ over 46 problems. Manual inspection finds violations that
survive the filter, so even that residual is a spot check rather than an estimate.

Table~\ref{tab:dose} gives the dose response in full. Two outcomes are scored on the same
generations. Answer recall is reproduction of the deleted parent's original answer;
procedure reproduction is the fraction of the parent's intermediate calculator results
appearing in the generation, excluding the final answer. Both are differences in
differences, injected minus held out at a dose, minus the same quantity at dose zero, so the
8{,}388{,}608 tokens every arm trains on cancel. The dose zero row is the reference rather
than a result, and its positive controls fail by construction, since a model that was never
shown the documents cannot have memorised them.

\section{Robustness of the licence to its wording}
\label{app:reword}

The result in Section 3.1 is produced by one sentence, so it is worth knowing how much of it
belongs to that sentence. The licence was reworded to carry the same three instructions,
work stepwise, mark the final answer with the same token, and decline with
\texttt{\#\#\#\# unanswerable} when the problem does not fix a unique answer, in different
words. The abstention token is held fixed so the scorer is unchanged between conditions and
any movement is attributable to wording rather than to what is counted. All 3179 pruned
items and all 1779 answerable parents were regenerated on all four checkpoints.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering

Two things separate. The developmental claim is robust to the wording: the ladder is monotone
under both licences, in the same order, with every interval excluding zero, and false declines
stay below one percent throughout the reworded condition. The level is not. Discrimination at
instruct falls from $+52.63$ to $+43.77$ points and declining on underdetermined items falls
from 0.538 to 0.444, so the rates quoted in Section 3.1 should be read as specific to the
licence that produced them rather than as properties of the checkpoint. The gap between
conditions widens as the checkpoint improves, running $-1.03$, $-3.46$, $-4.66$ and $-8.87$
points, so the checkpoints that verify best are also the most sensitive to how they are asked.
Truncation does not explain the shift, being equal or lower under the reworded licence at
every checkpoint, and markedly lower at stage one, at 0.133 against 0.211 on pruned items.

The same rewording was applied to the cross family sweep of Section 3.3, on the identical
items (Table~\ref{tab:reword_fam}). All four instruct checkpoints still discriminate with
every interval excluding zero, and the ordering by decision quality is unchanged, so the
comparison in Section 3.3 does not depend on the licence used to obtain it. Levels fall
under rewording here as well. The false decline rate is the cell that moves most and it does
not move uniformly: Qwen2.5 3B rises from 0.043 to 0.188 and OLMo 2 falls from 0.292 to
0.191, while Instella stays near zero at 0.006. The spread in how models respond to the
wording of a licence is therefore wider than the spread in how well they discriminate, which
is a further reason to report false refusal separately rather than folding it into a single
rate.

\section{Reproduction}

All generation, containment, and analysis code, together with every scored generation
file and containment record underlying this work, is released for audit at a public
repository and a paired dataset mirror, both anonymized for review and to be
de anonymized on acceptance. Every containment number reported uses the maximum over
individual documents definition stated above; an earlier internal computation of the
same quantity against the corpus pooled as one text produced a materially different
figure for the same released split and is superseded by the document level definition
throughout this work. Random seeds are fixed across item selection, deletion variant
construction, the continued pretraining mixture, and every cluster bootstrap. An
assistant was used to help implement generation, containment, and analysis code under
the direction of the authors, who independently verified every reported number against
the underlying generation files; the assistant did not design the probes or select which
results to report.
